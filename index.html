How do gross and net numbers differ?

Net receipts are reported after tax refunds. Net outlays are reported after applicable offsetting receipts and collections. These adjustments explain why gross amounts can exceed the headline totals.

FY2026 figures shown on another explorer: $5.99T gross receipts, $583B refunds, and $8.11T gross outlays. We have not independently verified those three component figures against the corresponding Treasury detail tables, so they are intentionally not presented as confirmed statistics here.

Verified FY2026 net totals: $5.403T receipts, $7.396T outlays, and $1.992T deficit. See the congressional fiscal update ↗.

Sources and methodology

Treasury's Monthly Treasury Statement provides actual federal receipts, outlays and the deficit. USAspending provides award obligations, which are a different measurement. World Bank GDP is an economy-wide measure. No comparison of GDP minus government spending is labeled a government “net gain” or “loss,” because that would be misleading. Recent years can be revised. An incomplete fiscal year is excluded from annual Treasury history.

Sources: U.S. Treasury · USAspending API · World Bank GDP
